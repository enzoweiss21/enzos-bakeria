ENZO'S BAKERY - CHANGES FROM THE DESIGN DOCUMENT (ONE-PAGER)
==========================================================

The core of the one-pager is all here: a 2D pixel-art bakery in one room, one baker,
three baked goods, customers who order and wait with patience bars, ingredients ->
prepare -> bake -> serve, money, and a workday that gets busier as it goes. Everything
listed under "Scope" was built. These are the things that changed along the way, and why.


1. ONE WORKDAY BECAME THREE (still one bakery)
   One-pager: "One full workday" with a money goal.
   Now: a short campaign of three days. Every day happens in the same bakery (no new
   levels or environments, as planned), but each day adds something new:
     Day 1  Bread and Cookies only, with tutorial hints.            Goal $75
     Day 2  Cupcakes (and the frosting step), customers in a hurry. Goal $120
     Day 3  Two-item orders, and a food critic at lunchtime.         Goal $180
   Why: with one day, the game was over as soon as it got interesting. Three days give
   a difficulty curve, and the upgrades (below) needed a "between days" to be bought in.
   If you miss a day's goal you retry that day.

2. THE UPGRADES MADE IT IN
   One-pager: "If I finish everything else early, I might add a couple upgrades like
   making the oven faster or making customers more patient."
   Now: an upgrade shop between days with six upgrades, paid for with the money you
   earned: Turbo Ovens (faster oven), Comfy Chairs (more patient customers), Third
   Oven, Quick Whisk (mix and frost faster), Running Shoes, and Tip Jar.

3. CONTROLS (same keys, slightly different jobs)
   One-pager: WASD to move, E to interact with stations and customers, Space to pick up
   and put down.
   Now: WASD (or arrow keys) to move. Space picks up, puts down AND serves food. E takes
   orders and pets the cat, and HOLDING E is how you mix dough and frost cupcakes.
   At the counter, Space and E both work, so players never press the "wrong" key there.
   Esc pauses. Why: with the original split, E and Space would often have done the same
   thing at a station. Now the rule is simple: Space = "move an item", E = "do something".
   A highlight around the targeted station and a prompt at the bottom of the screen
   ("[Space] Take flour") show what will happen.

4. THE "PREPARATION STATION" IS NOW MIXING BOWLS, PLUS A FROSTING STATION
   One-pager: ingredient station -> preparation station -> oven -> customer, with simple
   recipes.
   Now: recipes stayed simple, as planned. Everything is flour plus at most one topping:
     flour             -> bread dough
     flour + chocolate -> cookie dough
     flour + berries   -> cupcake batter (bake it, then frost it)
   There are three ingredient crates, two mixing bowls, two ovens (a third can be
   bought), a frosting station, a trash can, and plain counters plus an island for
   setting things down. Cupcakes got the extra frosting step so that the third item
   actually plays differently from the other two.

5. TAKING ORDERS (inspired by Papa's Bakeria)
   Customers arrive with a "!" and you have to greet them (E) to see what they want.
   Their patience drains faster until you do. This adds one more thing to juggle when
   several customers come in at once, which was the challenge the one-pager wanted.

6. BAKING HAS TIMING NOW
   One-pager: "a simple baking system."
   Now: when the oven dings there's a short golden PERFECT window. Take the food out
   right then for a bonus tip. Leave it too long and the oven starts beeping, then the
   food burns and has to go in the trash. Dough taken out early keeps its progress.
   Why: waiting for an oven was dead time. The timing gives you something to watch for.

7. HOW MONEY WORKS
   One-pager: "Every order you finish gives you money."
   Now: each item has a price (bread $10, cookies $6, cupcake $12). On top of that the
   customer tips based on how much patience they had left, +$2 for each perfect bake,
   and serving 3+ happy customers in a row builds a streak bonus. Each day ends with a
   results screen (served, lost, perfect bakes, tips, best streak, 1-3 stars).

8. DIFFERENT CUSTOMERS
   One-pager: "Different customers" (art) and "different customer orders."
   Now: every customer is randomly assembled from body, shirt, pants, hair, beard and
   hat sprites, so they all look different. Each has their own voice pitch for their
   little babble. There are also customer types: regulars, people in a hurry (very
   impatient, big tips), grandmas and grandpas (patient, small tips), and the food
   critic on Day 3 (only perfect bakes impress them, but then they pay triple).

9. NEW: BISCUIT THE BAKERY CAT
   Not in the one-pager. A cat wanders around the kitchen. Pet her (E) and every
   waiting customer gets some patience back. Then she naps for a while, so you have to
   save her for the right moment.

10. MUSIC IS TIED TO THE GAMEPLAY
   One-pager: "some simple background music playing while the game is running."
   Now: instead of a looping track, the music is generated live in code and follows the
   bakery. It starts as just a bass line in the quiet morning. A piano joins when a
   customer walks in, a shaker at 2+ waiting customers, and the melody during the lunch
   rush. The tempo speeds up with every waiting customer, and a ticking clock comes in
   when someone is about to storm out or something is about to burn. It's calm in the
   menus and quieter while paused. The cafe chatter in the background also gets louder
   the more customers are inside.

11. SOUND
   Everything the one-pager listed is in: oven (door, crackling, ding, warning beeps,
   burning), preparation (flour, mixing/whisking, frosting), customer sounds (door bell,
   voices, happy and angry), cash register, order completed, and sounds for mistakes
   and lost customers. Added: footsteps, the cat, a tip jar, jingles for the start and
   end of the day, and menu clicks.

12. A WORKDAY'S LENGTH AND RUSH HOUR
   The clock runs from 8 AM to 4 PM in 2.5 real minutes. It's slow in the morning, a
   lunch rush hits at 11:30, "last call" is at 3:30 (no new customers), and the day
   ends once the last customer has been served or has left.

13. SMALL THINGS
   - Day 1 has tutorial hints that follow what you're actually doing.
   - The sky in the windows changes from sunrise to sunset as the day goes on.
   - The character art only faces forward, so walking is animated with a hop and a
     lean instead of walk-cycle frames.
   - The game pauses itself if the window loses focus, so nothing burns while you're away.
