Enzo's Bakery - Changes From My Design Document

Overall the game ended up pretty close to what I described in my one-pager. It's still one
bakery, one baker, three baked goods, customers with patience bars, and the basic loop of
getting ingredients, preparing them, baking, and serving before the customer gets tired of
waiting. But a few things changed once I actually started building and playing it.

The biggest change is that there are three days now instead of one. My plan was one full
workday, but once I played it, one day felt like it ended right when it was getting fun. So
I split it into three days that all happen in the same bakery. Day 1 only has bread and
cookies and shows tutorial hints. Day 2 adds cupcakes and customers who are in a hurry.
Day 3 adds orders with two items and a food critic who shows up at lunch. Each day has a
money goal ($75, $105, $160) and if you miss it you just replay that day.

I also got to the upgrades I said I might add if I had time. Between days there's a shop
where you spend the money you made. Besides the faster oven and more patient customers I
mentioned in the one-pager, I added a third oven, a whisk that mixes faster, running
shoes, and a tip jar.

The controls changed a little. I kept WASD, E and Space, but I gave them clearer jobs.
Space is for moving items around (picking up, putting down, serving) and E is for doing
something (taking an order, petting the cat). Holding E is how you mix dough and frost
cupcakes. At the counter both keys work so you can't really press the wrong one. I also
added an outline around whatever you're facing and a prompt at the bottom of the screen
that tells you what the button will do, because without it you couldn't tell what you
were about to interact with. Esc pauses and M turns the music off.

The "preparation station" turned into mixing bowls. To keep recipes simple like I planned,
everything is flour plus at most one topping: just flour makes bread, flour and chocolate
makes cookies, and flour and berries makes cupcakes. Cupcakes also have to be frosted after
baking so that the third item plays a little differently from the other two.

I added some timing to the oven. In the one-pager it was just "a simple baking system," but
waiting for the oven was boring. Now when it dings there's a short window where you can
take it out for a perfect bake and a bigger tip. If you leave it in too long it starts
beeping and then burns, and burnt food has to go in the trash.

A few other things I added along the way:
- You have to take a customer's order (press E) before you can see what they want. I got
  this idea from Papa's Bakeria and it gives you one more thing to juggle when a bunch of
  people show up at once.
- Customers tip more the faster you serve them, and serving several happy customers in a
  row gives a streak bonus. There's a results screen at the end of each day.
- There are different kinds of customers: regulars, people in a hurry, grandmas who are
  really patient, and the food critic who only cares about perfect bakes but pays triple.
  The customers are put together from random clothes and hair so they all look different.
- A bakery cat named Biscuit walks around the kitchen. If customers are getting impatient
  you can pet her and everyone waiting gets some patience back, but then she takes a nap
  for a while.
- I made the customers wait a lot longer than I first had them. With the original wait
  times it was basically impossible to keep up.

For sound, I had everything I listed (oven, prep sounds, customer sounds, cash register,
finishing an order, losing a customer) plus footsteps, the cat, and jingles at the start
and end of each day. The one thing I changed was the background music. Since the
assignment said music had to be tied to the gameplay, I made it change with what's going
on. It starts with just a bass line when the bakery is empty, more instruments come in as
more customers are waiting, it speeds up during the lunch rush, and a ticking sound comes
in when someone is about to leave or something is about to burn. The background chatter
also gets louder when the bakery is fuller.

Last, some smaller stuff. The sky in the windows goes from morning to sunset as the day goes
on, there's a lunch rush around noon and a "last call" before closing, and the game pauses
itself if you click out of the window. The character art only faces forward, so instead of
walking animations the characters bounce a little when they move.
